# Fundamentals of Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the biological process used by plants, algae, and certain bacteria to convert light energy into chemical energy. During oxygenic photosynthesis, light energy is captured by photosynthetic pigments—primarily chlorophyll—and used to convert water and carbon dioxide into glucose (sugars) and oxygen gas.

## Chemical Reaction

The overall chemical equation for oxygenic photosynthesis is:
6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2

Carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and water from the soil are transformed using captured solar photons into glucose, which serves as metabolic fuel and building blocks, while releasing oxygen as a byproduct.

## Light and Dark Reactions

1. **Light-Dependent Reactions**: Occur within the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts. Photons strike chlorophyll pigments in Photosystems II and I, exciting electrons through an electron transport chain. This drives photophosphorylation to produce ATP and NADPH while photolysis of water releases molecular oxygen.
2. **Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle)**: Occur in the stroma of chloroplasts. The enzyme RuBisCO fixes carbon dioxide onto ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP), utilizing ATP and NADPH generated by light reactions to synthesize three-carbon glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate (G3P), which forms glucose.
